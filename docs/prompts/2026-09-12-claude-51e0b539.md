```text
## What the product is

A small, private tool for you and a few trusted people that turns YouTube channels into a readable digest and a chat you can ask questions of. It runs entirely on Cloudflare, has a text-only UI, and identifies people by an email header with no login at all.

The central idea is **shared once, read privately**. There is one global catalog of channels. Every episode is transcribed, chunked, embedded, and summarised exactly once, and everyone who follows that channel reads the same summary. What stays private per person is their follows, what they have read, their chats, and their chat preferences.

## The five moving parts

- **Two kinds of storage.** One global Registry Durable Object holds users, channels, followers, episodes, summaries, discovery runs, and processing attempts. One User Durable Object per email holds follows, read receipts, chats, and preferences. They never join across each other in SQL. Vectorize holds transcript chunks in a single shared namespace.

- **Channels are an approval container, nothing more.** A channel is `requested`, `approved`, or `declined`. Anyone adds one by pasting a `UC…` id, which also follows them. Only the owner approves, declines, pauses, or resumes. Declining hides the channel from readers but deletes nothing, and either the owner re-approving or a user "requesting again" undoes it. A pause flag on approved channels stops feed checks. The system sets it when the last follower leaves and the owner can set it by hand. Channel status never records whether imports worked.

- **Episodes carry the real state machine.** An episode is `pending`, `available`, `failed`, or `skipped`. Discovery is one thing: a cron or first approval reads the RSS feed and creates new episode rows. Processing is another: every episode attempt is one row in an attempts ledger and one Cloudflare Workflow instance. Attempts fetch the transcript from DownSub, chunk, embed, summarise, and publish. Anything that does not succeed and is not a deterministic skip retries every six hours for one 48-hour window, then becomes `failed INGESTION_TIMEOUT` and lands in the owner's Needs attention list. Deterministic skips are short videos, non-English captions, unplayable videos, and an owner skip. Recovery keeps running even when the channel is paused or declined.

- **Follows are written twice.** The User DO's follow row is what a person sees as their list. The Registry keeps a mirror follower record so it can count followers, show the owner who is waiting on a requested channel, and auto-pause a channel nobody follows.

- **Digest, unread, and chat.** The digest is summaries from eligible channels, meaning active follows intersected with approved channels, ordered by when the summary first became available. A summary is unread until it is actually returned to that person. Chat is global per user: every message searches all their eligible channels, retrieves the best three chunks with a channel filter, and answers with video and timestamp citations. Following a new channel widens existing chats. Unfollowing or a decline narrows future retrieval but never scrubs history.
```

```text
I think you should remove it, and for both recovery modes, not only publication. It is a premature optimization that saves almost nothing and has already cost the spec two exceptions.

**What it saves.** The concern was noise: hundreds of episodes blocked for the same reason, each writing a row per tick. An episode can be blocked at most nine times, once at discovery and once per six-hourly tick, and then it is failed and writes nothing more. For the PRD's Home target of 20 channels, assuming one video a day each:

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Episodes in recovery at any moment | about 40 |
| Blocked rows per episode, worst case | 9 |
| Rows for a full month of zero credits | about 5,400 |
| Storage for those rows | about 1 MB |

DO SQLite and Workers Paid billing do not notice that. Status calls are free and happen once per batch regardless, so removing the optimization adds no DownSub traffic.

**What it costs.** The spec started with one rule, "a blocked start writes no row", and has been patching it since. Owner Retry became an exception on 2026-09-12 so the owner gets a durable answer.
```
