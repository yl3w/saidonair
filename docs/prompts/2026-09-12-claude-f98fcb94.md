### Replied · 2026-09-12 16:44

```text
Names that fit what the product actually is: a text-only reader that turns the YouTube channels a small trusted group follows into digests, with timestamped citations and chat.

**Reading instead of watching**

- **Unwatched**. The read/unread state, and the whole premise of consuming videos without watching them.
- **Plaintext**. Video turned into plain text, and a literal description of the UI.
- **Readback**. Radio term for repeating what was heard. Here it is reading your channels back.
- **Teletext**. The old text-only TV information service, which is exactly the vibe. Trademark risk in some countries.

**The written record**

- **Minutes**. Meeting minutes are the written record of what was said. It also nods to minutes into the video for citations.
- **Rundown**. The broadcast term for the ordered list of a show's segments. A digest is a rundown of your channels.
- **Shorthand**. Condensed writing of speech, which is what a summary of a transcript is.
- **Abridged**. The shorter version of every episode.
- **Liner Notes**. The text that ships with a recording.
- **Précis**. A formal, literary word for a concise summary.

**Channels and conversation**

- **Backchannel**. A private side conversation about channels among a small trusted group. It leans on the chat feature.
- **Crosstalk**. Chat that spans every channel you follow. Also a broadcast term.
- **Playbill**. The printed program listing the shows and tonight's episodes.
- **Commonplace**. A commonplace book is a personal compilation of excerpts with their sources. Fits the long-lived personal tool framing.

My pick is **Minutes**, with **Rundown** as runner-up. Both are broadcast or record-keeping words that describe the digest without saying "digest", and both carry a second meaning that matches the product. If you want the name to sell the chat and small-group side instead, **Backchannel** is the strongest.

Two caveats. I did not check domain or trademark availability for any of these. Common-word names are also hard to search for, which matters little for a personal tool but would matter if it ever went public.
```
