### Replied · 2026-09-13 14:12

```text
Almost none from the batch sizes themselves. The numbers that reach a reader are the ones around them.

**The batch sizes.** The pipeline embeds 20 chunks per Workers AI call and upserts each batch as it goes, so the upsert ceiling of 200 never comes into play. Reading back a generation for verification uses 20 ids per call: a one-hour episode is about 60 chunks and three calls, a three-hour episode about ten calls, each a sub-second round trip. Deleting a previous generation is one call for any realistic episode. Taken together these add well under a second to an attempt that already spends seconds on the transcript and nine or so on the summary. Nobody sees it.

**What a reader does feel** is Vectorize's eventual consistency, which the probe measured at ten to eighty seconds before a written vector can be read back. Because availability is published only after the whole generation is verified, an episode stays `pending` and its attempt stays `running` for that extra minute or two after the summary is already generated. The owner's detail screen shows the attempt running for longer than the work took.

**The one tuning that matters** sits in M3.5, not here. Its verify step retries eight times with exponential spacing from five seconds, so the checks land at roughly 5, 15, 35, 75 and 155 seconds. With an eighty-second lag, verification succeeds on the fifth check, about two and a half minutes after the upsert, when the data was ready at eighty seconds. A gentler schedule, such as a check every ten seconds for the first few minutes and backing off only after that, would publish most episodes a minute sooner at the cost of a few more cheap reads. I would make that change when M3.5 is built.

**One second-order effect** is on the related-episodes list. M3.5 queries the top 20 neighbouring chunks and keeps at most five distinct videos. When one neighbouring episode contributes many similar chunks, twenty candidates may cover fewer than five videos and the list comes back short. Raising that query to the cap of 50 costs nothing meaningful and would fill the list more often. Also an M3.5 decision.

Nothing here touches DownSub credits or AI spend, and none of it changes what the digest shows once an episode is available.
```
