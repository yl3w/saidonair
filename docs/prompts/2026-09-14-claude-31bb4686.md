```text
Good question to sit with, because I think the honest answer is "probably not, and the fact that we're asking points at a bigger lever."

## Direct answer: chaining

**What it buys you** — it fixes both remaining faults at their source. Section 2 knows who was introduced in section 1, so "the speaker" becomes "Danielle Smith". And section 2 knows what's already been said, so it stops re-making section 1's point. Right now we're trying to fix duplication at the reduce, which is the wrong place: the reduce can only dedupe what it can recognise as duplicate, and it's already shown it won't.

**What it costs you**

1. **Poisoning.** A bad section 1 contaminates everything downstream. Today a bad section is one bad section out of three and the reduce can outvote it. Chained, it's the premise for every call that follows. This is the standard failure mode of refine chains and it's worse for us than most, because our failure mode isn't "wrong", it's "vague" — and vagueness propagates invisibly.
2. **Anchoring.** The model becomes conservative. Once it's written a narrative it tends to confirm it, under-reporting material in section 3 that cuts against section 1. For a debate show — which is half your catalog — that's exactly backwards.
3. **Debuggability.** Section 3's output stops being a function of section 3. You can't re-run one section, can't isolate a regression, can't A/B a prompt change cleanly. That matters more than it sounds when you're iterating on prompts.
4. **It forecloses parallelism.** Note the map calls are *already* sequential — a plain `await` in a `for` loop — so chaining costs no wall-clock today. But it means we can never parallelize them. For a 3-hour podcast that's 4 calls × 8 seconds we'd be giving up.

Of those, only #1 and #2 are real quality risks. #3 and #4 are engineering comfort.

## The reframe I'd push

The question isn't *whether* to feed something forward, it's **what**. Two very different things get conflated:

- **Positive context** — "here's the story so far, continue it." This is what invites drift and anchoring.
- **Negative context** — "these points are already covered, don't repeat them." The model isn't asked to build on it or believe it, just to avoid it. Tiny blast radius.

And **static context** — "here is who is in this episode" — is a third thing entirely, and it's the one that actually solves attribution. It doesn't need to come from the previous section at all.

That splits your problem cleanly:

| fault | best fix | serial? |
|---|---|---|
| "the speaker" | a **static header** — title + participants, extracted once, given to every section identically | no |
| duplicate takeaways | previous sections' **takeaway texts only**, as a do-not-repeat list | yes |

The header is strictly better than chaining for attribution: interviews introduce people in the first two minutes, so one cheap extraction — or just the episode title plus the first ~90 seconds verbatim — gives every section the cast list, deterministically, in parallel, with no drift path. Chaining would get you the same names by a much riskier route.

So: **no to chaining as such. Yes to a static header. Maybe to a narrow do-not-repeat list**, and only because we're already serial so it's free.

## The bigger lever

Before we build any of that, I'd want to know: **does the whole episode fit in one call?**

From the probe in this session, the 10-minute fixture was 13,029 characters of prompt — call it ~1,300 chars a minute, so roughly 15k tokens for a 45-minute section. A 90-minute episode is ~30k tokens. Llama 3.3 supports far more than that natively; the open question is what Cloudflare's Workers AI actually caps context at for that model, and I don't know the number offhand.

If a 90-minute episode fits in one call, then for most of your catalog **sectioning, the reduce, attribution, and cross-section dedup all stop existing as problems.** We'd be deleting code rather than adding prompt rules. Sectioning becomes a fallback for genuinely long content — three-hour interviews — rather than the default path.

That's a half-hour probe against the live model and it dominates everything else on this list. I'd do it first and let the answer decide whether the header/dedup work is even needed.

## One product thought on the takeaways themselves

We've been treating takeaways as *summary* — "the most insightful claims". But they carry timestamps, and the reader clicks them. Functionally that list is a **table of contents**, not a set of insights.

If we prompted for it that way — "the 6 moments a reader would want to jump to, and why" — I suspect the definitions and the "is a complex issue" filler disappear on their own, because neither is a moment. It also makes "distinct" fall out naturally: two entries pointing at the same idea are obviously wrong in a table of contents, while they're merely redundant in a list of insights.

Cheaper than any of the above, and testable in one prompt edit.

## The part I'd insist on as your PM counterpart
```
