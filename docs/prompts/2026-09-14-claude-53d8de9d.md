Shorts don't just cost a DownSub call each — they **crowd out real episodes inside the 15-entry cap**. For All-In, `initial_import_count: 5` currently imports 4 Shorts and 1 episode. That's a live bug, and `UULF` fixes it.

## The catch

Live streams **do** appear in the channel feed (Sky News 7/15, NASA 7/15, PBS 4/15 — all confirmed present in `channel_id=`), and a stream stays in `UULV` **forever after it ends** — MKBHD's `UULV` is all finished VODs from 2013–2019, and LTT's WAN Show sits there. Today those are handled correctly: `LIVE_OR_UPCOMING` waits, the VOD's transcript appears, the episode publishes. Switching to `UULF` alone would silently drop that whole content class — podcasts that stream live are exactly our use case.

So the option I'd take is **discover from `UULF` + `UULV`, merged and sorted by `published`**. Two free unauthenticated GETs per channel per 6 h, PRD behaviour preserved exactly minus Shorts.

## It doesn't remove the classification logic

- `SHORT` is a **duration** rule (<180 s), not a "is it a Short" rule. A 2-minute regular landscape upload lives in `UULF` and still isn't worth summarizing. DownSub hands us the duration in the call we already make, so the check is free.
- `LIVE_OR_UPCOMING` is still needed for premieres, for streams caught while live, and for every `UULV` entry we'd now be merging in.
- Owner Retry runs `classify()` on any episode regardless of how it was discovered.

The win is DownSub credits (~30% of calls today are spent learning "this is a Short"), a cleaner ledger, and the crowding-out fix — not less code.

## Code impact (small, but not one line)

`apps/api/src/lib/youtube/rss.ts` needs four things beyond `feedUrl()`:

1. **Feed `<title>` becomes `"Videos"`**, not the channel name — `routes/channels.ts:141` (`title: body.title ?? feed.title`) would name channels "Videos". Fix: read `<author><name>`, which carries the real channel name in **both** feed shapes.
2. **No `<link rel="alternate">` in the playlist head.** `channelIdOf()` falls through to `<yt:channelId>`, which in playlist feeds carries the **full `UC` prefix** — the inverse of the quirk the file's comment documents. It works by luck; the comment needs correcting.
3. **404 semantics change.** `UULV` 404s for a channel that has never streamed (confirmed: Lex Fridman, Huberman) — that's "empty", not "no such channel". Today 404 → `null` → "feed unavailable". Channel *existence* should stay pinned to `channel_id=` at add time.
4. **`YOUTUBE_FEEDS_FAKE`** keys on `searchParams.get("channel_id")`; the seam and its fixtures need the playlist form.

One risk to name: these prefixes are undocumented. If YouTube retires them, `UULF` 404s and every discovery run silently reports "feed unavailable" — worth a fallback to `channel_id=` rather than trusting them blind.

### Replied · 2026-09-14 12:05

````text
Here's the full reasoning.

## 1. It isn't "long-form vs Shorts" — there are three buckets

The natural assumption is that `UULF` means "everything except Shorts." It doesn't. YouTube splits a channel's uploads into **three** disjoint auto-playlists, and I confirmed they partition the channel feed exactly — every entry landed in exactly one bucket, no overlap, nothing unclassified, across all 13 channels:

```
channel_id= feed  ==  UULF  ⊎  UUSH  ⊎  UULV
                      long    Shorts   live
```

So the complement of `UULF` is **Shorts *and* live**. Taking `UULF` alone drops two categories, not one.

## 2. `UULV` is a provenance bucket, not a "currently live" flag

This is the part that makes it permanent rather than temporary. A video lands in `UULV` because of **how it was published** — it was broadcast live — and it never migrates out when the stream ends. The proof is in the data:

- **Fireship's** `UULV`: four entries, newest **2018-10-10**
- **MKBHD's** `UULV`: six entries, 2013–2019
- **All-In's** `UULV`: the 2024 election night and 2025 inauguration livestreams, still sitting there

If videos graduated to `UULF` after going offline, those feeds would be empty. They aren't. **Once live, always `UULV`** — years later, as an ordinary watchable VOD with captions, indistinguishable from any other video on the channel page.

That's the crux: `UULV` is not a holding pen for streams in progress. It is the permanent home of a large class of finished, transcribable, summarizable content.

## 3. How much content that actually is

Of the non-Shorts entries in each channel's newest 15, here's the share sitting in the live bucket:

| Channel | long-form | live | live share of real content |
|---|---|---|---|
| PBS NewsHour | 1 | 4 | **80%** |
| VEGETTA777 | 6 | 9 | **60%** |
| Sky News | 6 | 7 | **54%** |
| NASA | 8 | 7 | **47%** |
| Lofi Girl | 3 | 2 | 40% |
| Lex Fridman, Huberman, Fireship, CNN, LTT, All-In, BBC, Bloomberg | — | 0 in recent 15 | 0% |

And the titles are exactly what this product exists to summarize:

- **Linus Tech Tips** — all 15 `UULV` entries are **WAN Show** episodes, their flagship weekly podcast
- **Bloomberg Television** — all 15 are full **Bloomberg Technology** episodes
- **CNN** — "Full White House briefing with VP Vance", "9/11 NYC commemoration: Full ceremony"
- **All-In Podcast** — "2024 Election Night Livestream with the Besties"
- **NASA** — Crew-12 press conference, Roman Telescope pre/post-launch briefings

Note LTT and Bloomberg show **0** in the recent-15 column but have 15 live entries each. Their live content is real and regular — it's just older than the window the 15-entry channel feed currently covers. The zero is an artifact of sampling, not an absence.

## 4. The failure would be silent

This is what tips it for me. Compare the two paths for the same WAN Show episode:

**Today:** discovery creates an episode → attempt runs → `waiting LIVE_OR_UPCOMING` → retried 6-hourly → the VOD's captions appear → **it publishes**. If it never resolves, at the 48 h deadline it becomes `failed INGESTION_TIMEOUT` and lands in **Needs attention**, visible to you.

**Under `UULF`-only:** the episode is never discovered. No episode row. No attempt. No `waitReason`. No Needs-attention entry. The run just reports a smaller `N episodes discovered` and looks perfectly healthy.

You would have no signal at all — you'd only find out by noticing a channel's digest was thinner than YouTube's page. And because discovery only creates entries *published after first approval*, by the time you spotted it the missed episodes would be outside the window and not recoverable without a manual backfill.

Contrast that with the Shorts case, which is safe to drop precisely because dropping it loses nothing you wanted.

## 5. Why not subtract instead?

"Read the channel feed, subtract `UUSH`" also needs two fetches, and it's strictly worse: the channel feed's **15-entry cap is still shared with Shorts**, so for All-In you'd fetch 15 and keep 4. `UULF` + `UULV` gives you up to **30 slots that contain only real content** — which also fixes the crowding-out problem for busy channels and widens the effective discovery window. Same request count, better coverage.

## 6. What it costs

One extra unauthenticated GET per channel per discovery run — free, no quota, no API key, and discovery already walks channels one at a time. On a 6-hourly cron with a handful of channels, it's nothing.

## The honest counter-case

If every channel you follow is Lex-Fridman-shaped — never streams, `UULV` empty or ancient — then `UULF` alone behaves identically and the extra fetch buys nothing. That describes 8 of the 13 channels I sampled.

But you can't know which shape a channel is when you approve it, and channels change: LTT added WAN Show, any podcast can start streaming next week, and a one-off election-night special is exactly the episode you'd most want summarized. The merged read costs one free GET to make that whole category impossible to miss.

One thing I did **not** verify: whether a *premiere* (pre-recorded upload with a scheduled debut) stays in `UULF` or moves to `UULV`. I'd assume `UULF`, since it isn't a broadcast, but it's worth a probe before we commit — if premieres land in `UULV`, the argument for merging gets stronger still.
````
