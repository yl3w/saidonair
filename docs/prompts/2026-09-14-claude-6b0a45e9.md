```text
## On asking the owner to pick a role: no

The instinct behind the question is right, but a role gate is the wrong instrument for it.

- **It taxes the frequent act to serve the rare one.** Reading is daily; approving a channel is five seconds a week. A door-level choice charges the daily act for the weekly one.
- **The switch already exists.** Identity here is an email typed on `/`, with "Switch account" on every screen. An owner who wants the reader's-eye view types another email. A role picker is a second switcher for the same person — and two tabs already act as two people by design (§7).
- **Owner-ness attaches to objects, not to sessions.** *This* channel wants your review; *that* episode failed and only you can retry it. Those belong beside the object, not behind a mode.
- **It invents a state you then have to explain.** "Why is my queue empty?" — "you're in reader mode" is a bug report waiting to happen.

What the question is right about: owner work is dense and tabular, and merging it into a reading app ruins both. The answer is **spatial separation** — one destination with its own density — not modal state. So: one nav, and for the owner one extra item plus controls in place. ```text
```
```text
It came from the founding document, not from this redesign — and it was never argued.

**Where each half comes from**

- **24 hours** is in the very first PRD, in the paragraph that defines the product: *"Today's digest. All summaries published in the last 24 hours across all channels, newest first… Viewing the digest marks those summaries read. Empty state: 'Nothing new since yesterday.'"* The window is baked into the word *digest* — a daily bulletin you sit down and read.
- **7 days** arrived later, in `5d330ff` with the Home read-experience spec (2026-09-07), purely as an escape hatch: a "Show last 7 days" button and a server-side clamp. `apps/api/src/routes/digest.ts:15` — `MAX_WINDOW_MS = 7 * DAY_MS`, with `since` defaulting to `now - DAY_MS` and clamped.

**What was never decided.** There is no §9 entry arguing for a window at all. The only recorded decision that touches it (2026-09-10) changed what the window *measures* — publication time → `summaryAvailableAt` — and the empty-state copy. Whether a time window is the right way to decide what a reader sees has never been on the table.

**Why it's now load-bearing in a way it wasn't.** The window made sense for a page that expands every summary inline: it's a bulletin, and a bulletin has an edition. Two things have changed under it. Episodes arrive on YouTube's schedule, not yours — a Tuesday with four long episodes and a Wednesday with none both get one "edition". And because the clamp is 7 days, an episode you didn't get to is not merely lower down; it is **unreachable from the queue** and only findable on its channel page. That's the failure Instapaper exists to prevent, and it's the same seam the Archive question landed on: an item leaves the queue by the clock, so a button that promises *you* decide when it leaves is promising something the window contradicts.

So: 24h/7d is inherited premise, and it costs a constant, a query default, and some copy to change. Nothing structural depends on it except the receipt-on-return rule you already overturned.

Worth deciding deliberately now, since the Design phase spec is written on top of it:

| | |
|---|---|
| **Keep the window** | The queue is a bulletin. Archive is really "Done" (a read receipt), and unread items aging out is intended behaviour. |
| **Drop the window** | The queue is an inbox: everything unarchived, oldest kept, no clock. Archive becomes real and needs `archived_at` plus a state-based digest route. |
| **Window as a filter, inbox as the model** | Default view "Last 24 hours" is a *lens* on an inbox that never loses anything — the queue keeps items until archived, and the time filter is one of the chips beside Unread/Everything. |

My recommendation is the third: it keeps the daily-bulletin rhythm you designed the product around, without the clock quietly throwing away what you didn't get to. It costs the same `archived_at` as the second.
```
