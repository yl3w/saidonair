```text
Checked all four against the nav. Short answer: **yes on Queue, Sources and Curate — no on History.**

**Where it actually repeats.** The nav is always on screen: the top bar is `sticky top-0` with Queue · Sources (Curate at ≥1024px for the owner), and below 768px those destinations move to a `fixed` bottom tab bar that prints the same words under their icons. The current one is marked in words (`aria-current`), weight, and a rule. So on `/queue` a reader sees "Queue" twice within 56px, and scrolling never takes either away — the usual defence for a page title ("the nav scrolled off") doesn't apply here.

It's also the rule this product keeps applying to itself. "Unread" left every queue row for being a constant; the reading-time estimate went the same way; so did `structured` in Curate's table and the three-zero Needs you section. *A value that cannot vary is not information* — and a page that names itself after you pressed its name in the nav is that exact shape. It's the row decision one level up: a row is identified by what its list does not already say; a screen should say what the frame does not already say.

**History is the exception, and not an awkward one.** It isn't in either nav — no top link, no tab — and it's reached by a link from the queue's ending and from a day heading. Its h1 is the only place it's named. On a day page that h1 is already the day (`15 September 2026`), which is content, not a label. So it keeps a visible title under the same rule that removes the other three.

**Two things I'd insist on if we do it:**

1. **Hide it, don't delete it.** Make the h1 `sr-only`. A document whose first heading is an h2 day group has no name for anyone navigating by headings, and the nav's `aria-current` doesn't substitute — it says where you are in a list of links, not what this document is. That's the §7 floor, not a nicety.
2. **The tab title is the thing actually missing.** `index.html` sets `<title>Said on Air</title>` once and no route ever changes it, so every tab, bookmark and history entry is identically named today. If the on-screen name goes, per-route `document.title` ("Queue · Said on Air") is what reclaims it — and it's worth doing whether or not you remove the headings.

**The cost, stated fairly:** on Queue and History the h1 carries `mr-auto` and is what pushes the channel filter and "Browse by date" to the right. Hide it and those controls need their own alignment, and that band becomes a toolbar with nothing to its left. On Sources the tab row ("Following / All") would lead instead, on Curate the section links — both read fine to me, arguably better, since the first thing on screen becomes something you can act on. On the queue you'd gain about 40px at the top of a phone, where the first day heading and first summary would lead.

Want me to make the change — `sr-only` h1 on Queue, Sources and Curate, History left visible, plus the per-route `document.title`? It's a `docs/design.md` §3 rule and a PRD §9 entry as well as the three screens.
```

### Replied · 2026-09-15 18:17

````text
It's a Tailwind utility — you already use it in five places. `apps/web/src/components/Nav.tsx:80` is the clearest: the account link shows an email (only at ≥1024px) and an avatar, so below that width it would be a link made of nothing but an image. `<span class="sr-only">Account</span>` gives it a name a screen reader announces, without putting the word on screen.

What the class compiles to:

```css
position: absolute;
width: 1px; height: 1px;
padding: 0; margin: -1px;
overflow: hidden;
clip-path: inset(50%);
white-space: nowrap;
border-width: 0;
```

The element is still rendered and still in the accessibility tree — it's just clipped to a pixel nobody can see. That's the whole trick, and it's why the three ways of hiding are not interchangeable:

| | On screen | Announced |
|---|---|---|
| `hidden` / `display: none` | no | **no** — gone for everyone |
| `sr-only` | no | yes |
| `aria-hidden="true"` | yes | no — for decoration, like the `·` in a meta line |

So `sr-only` on the h1 means the queue's document still says "Queue" as its top-level heading — anyone navigating by headings, or asking "what page is this", gets an answer — while the column starts visually at the first day. Delete the h1 instead and that answer doesn't exist anywhere: the page's first heading becomes an h2 day group, and the tab still says "Said on Air" like every other route.

One caveat worth knowing, since it's the usual way this pattern goes wrong: `sr-only` text is real text. It gets found by in-page search, copied with a selection, and translated. That's fine for a word like "Queue"; it's the reason you wouldn't use it to stash a paragraph of explanation.
````
